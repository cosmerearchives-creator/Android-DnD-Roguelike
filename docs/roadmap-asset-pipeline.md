## Implementation sequence and acceptance gates

1. **Finish recovery and harvesting audit.** Record rigs, joint tracks, range, loops, provenance and license per source. Audit weapons, shields, armor, clothing, accessories, tools, materials and textures on every import. Record each candidate as immediately reusable, minor preparation, manual repair/reconstruction, or not reasonably separable. Preserve the equipped derivative if useful and repair exposed geometry on a derivative where practical. Check duplicates by content hash and catalog identity.
2. **Define the master humanoid contract.** Start from Godot 4.4's [56-slot humanoid profile](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/classes/class_skeletonprofilehumanoid.html). The handoff's subtotal is 53, with face slots for both eyes and jaw accounting for the difference. Verify canonical names, parentage, axes, reference pose, meter scale, forward direction, root/hips convention, motion scale and optional bone handling in Godot 4.4.1. Map viable existing rigs with BoneMap, keep native proportions, and allow extra anatomy under mapped parents. Verify the same in-place locomotion clip on two differently proportioned humanoids.
3. **Prove shared animation playback.** Store reusable clips as family AnimationLibrary resources independent of meshes. Prototype AnimationPlayer and AnimationTree with an in-place locomotion base and filtered upper-body layers for approved actions. Gameplay owns velocity, attack timing and action economy. Validate rest transforms, loop seams, feet, grip, two-hand alignment and track binding. Recover clips before filling gaps.
4. **Prototype modular equipment and catalog.** Use sockets for rigid gear and compatible weights/body-fit variants for fitted items. Define hands, back, hips, head, chest, shoulders, wrists, waist and feet. Capture stable ID, source digest, license/credit, category, transform, socket, rig/fit support, materials, processing and independent review status. Map equipped visuals and eventual drops to existing item definitions.
5. **Establish library layout.** Proposed sibling `Master Asset Library` stores editable derivatives and working material by category. Keep originals and supporting bundles in their Beastiary workflow directories. Keep reproducible catalog and tooling in the repo and only game-ready resources under `godot/assets/`. Catalog creatures, skeletons, animations, weapons, shields, armor, clothing, accessories, tools, materials/textures and environment assets. Use IDs and relative source references plus hashes so moving originals cannot affect runtime loads.
6. **Build the developer Performance Lab and benchmark on Android.** Main-menu entry, fixed environment/camera, deterministic spawn layout and warm-up, then repeated and sustained runs. Try counts 0/1/5/10/20/50/100, mixed models, static/animated, quality variants, shadows, LOD and AI toggles. Log device, build, renderer, resolution and test settings, FPS, median and tail frame times, memory, draw calls, triangles, loading, animation cost where measurable and sustained thermal behavior. Isolate geometry, texture resolution/compression, materials, skeletons, blend layers, equipment, environment and AI. Test triangle tiers 2–5k, 5–20k, 20–60k and 60–150k+, independently varying 512–4096+ textures. Treat 60/45/30 FPS as provisional preferred/heavy/minimum targets. Derive budgets from real device results. Prefer asset lifetime, culling and sharing improvements before compression, shadow/LOD changes and geometry or texture reductions.
7. **Convert the Goblin Warrior pilot and then player rigs.** Keep original artwork and compare original-quality and optimized derivatives. Expand the existing Bestiary style with clip selection, equipment/alignment, deformation, complexity/performance and review information. Load only the selected creature. Validate gameplay compatibility, visual quality and equipment independently.
8. **Automate and validate a single complete import.** Discover source bundles and dependencies, archive/audit, harvest and classify, map/rig and weight, retarget, attach, validate, benchmark, optimize if measured, catalog and integrate game-ready copies. Use repeatable manifests and report manual repair cases. Keep failures in Imports. After verified integration, move originals and all dependencies from `Beastiary Imports` to `Beastiary Review` with pre/post hashes, duplicate collision checks and a resumable movement record. Preserve ZIPs, textures, materials, licenses and supporting files. Move originals to `Beastiary Complete` only after explicit approval of that model. Equipment approval is independent.
9. **Batch only after the complete single-asset workflow and Android budgets pass.** Expand later to extended humanoid, quadruped, draconic, serpentine, avian, arachnid and amorphous families.

The master rig, shared library, harvesting, lab and automated imports are proposed work. The first authorized implementation milestone is the recovery inventory and a two-model humanoid retargeting proof.


## Phase 1 recovery audit

The 32 incoming GLBs and the one review GLB were inspected through their metadata. None contains a skin or animation tracks. The original Goblin Warrior exposes 43,727 position entries under one mesh primitive and one material. Separate equipment objects are unavailable in that file. Connected components, UV islands and visual inspection are still needed before deciding whether equipment can be extracted. The separate motion-capture goblin contains a single mesh with distinct body and knife materials. Material-based knife selection is plausible and still needs geometry and license checks.

### Motion-capture archive

- Source is Danimal's Blend Swap entry 77353, Goblin animated by Motion capture. Archive SHA-256 is `b1853b31d7209e9ef0d7218158b2061adba1f789e1bec941ea058474e5c2ee9b`.
- Bundled HTML identifies CC BY 3.0 for the upload, attributes the goblin model to xGhostx7 under CC0, and identifies a CC-BY knife by Windastella. It does not identify the underlying mocap dataset. Verify downstream provenance before shipping any derived clip. Keep the ZIP and license intact.
- Blender evaluates the source at 24 fps. Walk-fast, Run-flee and Attack-two handed.002 each contain 96 curves targeting all 25 existing rig bones plus six absent finger/thumb targets. Discard unresolved targets in any derivative and inspect grip independently. The source has Hips, intermediary hip and neck bones and no dedicated Root or fingers. The canonical bone map must account for those intermediary transforms.
- Use NLA strip selections as the starting point for baking. Walk uses action frames 3–30, Flee 1–22, Attack1 68–133 and Attack2 188–255. The two attack strips share a single long action and are retimed in the scene. Preserve evaluated timing for a reference bake. Idle uses Attack-two handedAction frames 0–35 despite its name. The remaining strips cover sleep, dance, happy, sad and death.
- Sampled evaluated poses showed fixed hips through Walk and Flee with moving feet and hands, while both attacks produced substantial hand motion. Walk's sampled endpoints are closer than Flee's, but foot/hand offsets remain. Inspect the walk seam and foot contact before accepting it as a loop. The source description flags clipping. Evaluated transforms prove playback; full mesh review and Godot retargeting are pending.
